Logic

Connectives

\(p\)\(q\)\(p \land q\)\(p \lor q\)\(p \to q\)\(p \leftrightarrow q\)
FFFFTT
FTFTTF
TFFTFF
TTTTTT

\(\neg p\) flips the value. \(p \to q\) is false only when \(p\) is true and \(q\) is false. Precedence: \(\neg\), then \(\land\), then \(\lor\), then \(\to\), then \(\leftrightarrow\).

Logical equivalences

LawEquivalence
Identity\(p \land T \equiv p\),   \(p \lor F \equiv p\)
Domination\(p \lor T \equiv T\),   \(p \land F \equiv F\)
Idempotent\(p \land p \equiv p\),   \(p \lor p \equiv p\)
Double negation\(\neg\neg p \equiv p\)
Commutative\(p \land q \equiv q \land p\),   \(p \lor q \equiv q \lor p\)
Associative\((p \land q) \land r \equiv p \land (q \land r)\), same for \(\lor\)
Distributive\(p \land (q \lor r) \equiv (p \land q) \lor (p \land r)\)
\(p \lor (q \land r) \equiv (p \lor q) \land (p \lor r)\)
De Morgan\(\neg(p \land q) \equiv \neg p \lor \neg q\)
\(\neg(p \lor q) \equiv \neg p \land \neg q\)
Absorption\(p \lor (p \land q) \equiv p\),   \(p \land (p \lor q) \equiv p\)
Implication\(p \to q \equiv \neg p \lor q\)
Negated implication\(\neg(p \to q) \equiv p \land \neg q\)
Contrapositive\(p \to q \equiv \neg q \to \neg p\)
Biconditional\(p \leftrightarrow q \equiv (p \to q) \land (q \to p)\)

Rules of inference

These appear on the official formula sheet attached to the exam. The symbol \(\Rightarrow\) means “therefore”.

RulePattern
Modus ponens (detachment)\((p \to q) \land p \;\Rightarrow\; q\)
Modus tollens (indirect reasoning)\((p \to q) \land \neg q \;\Rightarrow\; \neg p\)
Disjunctive addition\(p \;\Rightarrow\; (p \lor q)\)
Conjunctive simplification\((p \land q) \;\Rightarrow\; p\)  and  \((p \land q) \;\Rightarrow\; q\)
Disjunctive simplification\((p \lor q) \land \neg p \;\Rightarrow\; q\)  and  \((p \lor q) \land \neg q \;\Rightarrow\; p\)
Hypothetical syllogism (chain rule)\((p \to q) \land (q \to r) \;\Rightarrow\; (p \to r)\)
Resolution\((\neg p \lor r) \land (p \lor q) \;\Rightarrow\; (q \lor r)\)

Conditional vocabulary

NameFormEquivalent to \(p \to q\)?
Converse\(q \to p\)No
Contrapositive\(\neg q \to \neg p\)Yes
Inverse\(\neg p \to \neg q\)No

“\(p\) is sufficient for \(q\)” means \(p \to q\). “\(p\) is necessary for \(q\)” means \(q \to p\). “\(p\) is necessary and sufficient” means \(p \leftrightarrow q\).

Sequences and series

SequenceRecursiveClosed form
Arithmetic, first term \(a\), difference \(d\)\(a_n=a_{n-1}+d\)\(a_n=a+dn\)  (if \(a_0=a\))
Geometric, first term \(a\), ratio \(r\)\(a_n=r\,a_{n-1}\)\(a_n=ar^n\)  (if \(a_0=a\))
Fibonacci\(a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}\), \(a_0=0,a_1=1\)Binet: \(\frac{1}{\sqrt5}\left[\left(\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right)^n-\left(\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}\right)^n\right]\)

Arithmetic series

First \(n+1\) terms:

\[ S_n=(n+1)a+\frac{dn(n+1)}{2}=\frac{(n+1)(2a+dn)}{2}. \]

Or \(\dfrac{\text{number of terms}}{2}(\text{first}+\text{last})\).

Geometric series

First \(n+1\) terms:

\[ S_n=\frac{a(1-r^{n+1})}{1-r}, \quad r \neq 1. \]

If \(|r|<1\): \(\displaystyle S_\infty=\frac{a}{1-r}\).

Notation: \(\sum_{k=m}^{n} a_k = a_m+\cdots+a_n\), starting value 0 for sums; \(\prod_{k=m}^{n} a_k = a_m\cdots a_n\), starting value 1 for products.

Caesar cipher

\(E_n(x)=(x+n) \bmod 26\)
\(D_n(x)=(x-n) \bmod 26\)
\(D_n=E_{26-n}\), so \(D_n \circ E_n=I\).
ROT13 is \(n=13\), self-inverse.

Standard domains: \(\sqrt{x}\) needs \(x \ge 0\); \(1/(x-c)\) needs \(x \neq c\); \(\log x\) needs \(x > 0\). Intervals: \([a,b]\) includes both ends, \((a,b)\) excludes both, \([a,b)\) includes only \(a\).

Binomial coefficients and Pascal

Binomial theorem

\[ (x+y)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}x^{n-k}y^{k}. \]

Coefficient of \(x^{n-k}y^k\) is \(\binom{n}{k}\).

Pascal's identity

\[ \binom{n}{k}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}+\binom{n-1}{k}. \]

Boundaries \(\binom{n}{0}=\binom{n}{n}=1\). Symmetry \(\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n}{n-k}\). Row sum \(\sum_k\binom{n}{k}=2^n\).

Bit-string recurrence: \(|B_n^k|=|B_{n-1}^{k-1}|+|B_{n-1}^{k}|\), with \(|B_n^0|=|B_n^n|=1\).

Reminder

Python's and/or return operands, not necessarily Booleans. Wrap in bool(...) if you need a strict True/False. Python sets are unordered and de-duplicate automatically; lists preserve order and duplicates; tuples and strings are immutable.

Top exam traps

Logic & sets

  • A false hypothesis makes an implication true, not false.
  • Converse \(\neq\) contrapositive.
  • “All \(A\) are \(B\)” is \(\forall[A \to B]\), not \(\forall[A \land B]\).
  • Negating “all \(A\) are \(B\)” gives “some \(A\) is not \(B\)”.
  • \(\forall\exists\) \(\neq\) \(\exists\forall\).
  • \(\{a\} \neq a\); \(\varnothing \neq \{\varnothing\}\).
  • \(A \setminus B = \varnothing\) means \(A \subseteq B\), not \(A=B\).
  • \(A \times B \neq B \times A\) in general.

Relations, functions, counting

  • Every function is a relation; not every relation is a function.
  • “Not symmetric” is not the same as antisymmetric.
  • “Not reflexive” is not the same as irreflexive.
  • \(f \circ g \neq g \circ f\) in general.
  • \(f^{-1}(x) \neq 1/f(x)\).
  • \(f^2(x)=f(f(x))\), not \((f(x))^2\).
  • Inverse exists only for bijections.
  • Do not add overlapping cases; subtract the intersection.
  • Order matters \(\Rightarrow\) permutation; order irrelevant \(\Rightarrow\) combination.
  • Remember the \(k!\) divisor when converting ordered lists to subsets.
  • Check counts against the size of the universe.
Final check

State the domain for quantified statements, state the source and target for relations and functions, and write one line of justification for every count. The method marks are usually worth more than the final number.
